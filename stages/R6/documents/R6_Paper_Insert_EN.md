# R6 development-study text (not a confirmatory main-result replacement)

## Acquisition accounting and replication
We audited an offline calibration experiment with four independently sampled training panels, each containing 16 catalogs. A single-effect learner acquired 82 canonical cells, a full learner acquired 182 cells, and selective learners appended 16 or 32 interaction cells. Each panel also required 80 reference-workflow executions. Standalone acquisition costs were therefore 1,392, 2,992, 1,648, and 1,904 workflow executions, respectively. The selective budgets were nested checkpoints, not independent training runs. Across all learners and panels, the training-stage ledger contained 40,384 executions, including 1,600 reference executions. This accounting excludes subsequent auditing executions and is neither a currency estimate nor an assumption that all workflows have equal computational cost.

Policies were evaluated on 128 frozen base streams under two paired probe menus, yielding 2,560 policy trajectories and 20,480 deterministic task evaluations. Independent arithmetic and trace reconstruction agreed with the recorded labels and results. Cross-platform replication preserved all discrete trajectory fields and all 20,480 recorded task-risk predictions. Fifty-seven acquisition diagnostic scores differed only at the floating-point tail (maximum absolute difference 3.795e-19), within the previously specified 1e-12 tolerance, without changing any selected cell. Replication does not increase the number of independent evaluation streams.

## Negative result and measured computational cost
Under the descriptive mixture of the four conditions and two menus, random acquisition with 32 added cells succeeded on 1,722 of 2,048 task evaluations, versus 1,712 for the decision-sensitive heuristic. Both used 1,975 runtime probe credits. With J defined as task failures plus 0.05 times runtime credits, the corresponding totals were 424.75 and 434.75; their per-policy-episode means were 1.65918 and 1.69824, respectively. These mixtures do not identify deployment-distribution effects. Nonzero episode-cost differences were confined to five base streams, and all eight condition-by-menu descriptive confidence intervals for this comparison included zero. The intervals are conditional on four fitted training panels and do not quantify population-level training or random acquisition-order uncertainty.

The decision-sensitive heuristic acquired fewer labels than the full learner but was slower on these inexpensive local workflows. For the returned machine, mean acquisition elapsed time at the 32-cell checkpoint was 0.42670 seconds for the decision-sensitive method, 0.10135 seconds for random acquisition, and 0.15490 seconds for full measurement. The first figure excludes a separately timed 0.08568-second reference-grid construction cost. Hence reducing the number of labels did not establish end-to-end acceleration. Comparisons must distinguish selector CPU time, acquisition elapsed time, grid construction, policy compilation, online execution, and any hypothetical amortized cost model.

## Supported conclusion and limitations
The experiment establishes auditable acquisition savings and a counterexample to the presumption that a more elaborate decision-sensitive selector necessarily offers a better cost–performance trade-off. It does not establish universal superiority of random acquisition. The tested heuristic evaluates two extreme values of a missing cell on a hypothetical grid of immediate decisions; it is not posterior expected value of information or full-horizon optimal experimental design. Known drift rates, specified task relevance, finite semantic modes, and idealized calibration remain assumptions. The environment is the existing synthetic catalog/inventory API, not an external benchmark. There were no new LLM/API calls and no new RepairLens experiments. Further claims require a materially new test of the relevant mechanism rather than repeating the same generator until a favorable result appears.
