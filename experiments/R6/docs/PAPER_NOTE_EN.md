# R6 manuscript evidence note — exploratory, not a replacement main result

## Paid calibration rather than access-count simulation
We distinguish the cost of learning a task-loss kernel from the cost of online refreshing. A training oracle executes a requested canonical semantic cell only after purchase. Public irrelevant-coordinate equivalences reduce the loss tensor to 82 single-flip and 100 interaction cells; each cell is measured on 16 catalogs, with 80 separately executed reference workflows per learner. Thus, standalone calibration requires 1,392 workflows for the single-effect model, 2,992 for the full model, and 1,648/1,904 for models with 16/32 additional measured cells. No selector accesses an unpurchased full tensor. Intermediate budgets are nested prefixes, not separate physical acquisitions.

## Acquisition and prior art
We compare random acquisition, current-action occupancy on a fixed public-state grid, and a two-extreme immediate-decision sensitivity heuristic. The last rule measures the regret of retaining the current action when one unknown loss entry is set to zero or one. It is not Bayesian value of information or a general dynamic active-learning guarantee. Decision-focused prediction and targeted information acquisition have substantial prior art (Javdani et al., AISTATS 2014; Mandi et al., ICML 2022; Filstroff et al., arXiv:2106.04193). Our contribution at this stage is the explicit acquisition ledger and controlled calibration-cost experiment, not a new general theorem.

## Observations
Four independent training panels and 128 independent evaluation streams are used; each stream is evaluated with paired probe menus and all ten policies, yielding 20,480 rule-executed task records. Under the shared matched condition, the 32-cell decision-sensitive model and full calibration both solve 208/256 task positions, but the random 16-cell baseline achieves the same score at lower calibration cost. Under the distribution shift, decision-sensitive calibration solves 224/256 versus 228/256 for single effects and 231/256 for random acquisition. These exploratory results do not establish superiority of the proposed acquisition heuristic. Conditional paired intervals touch zero in key contrasts and do not cover population-level training variability.

## Cost and limitations
Fewer acquired labels do not imply lower wall-clock cost: in the first complete local run, 32-step decision-sensitive acquisition takes approximately 2.45 seconds on average across panels versus 0.46 seconds for full calibration in this inexpensive simulator. Hypothetical amortization curves use explicitly stipulated label prices and deployment horizons; they are not billed API savings. The setting retains known drift, three semantic channels, exact initial calibration, and a deterministic compiler. It is not a new LLM benchmark, public environment transfer or general agent evaluation. Cross-machine repetition must not increase sample counts.

## Numerical reproducibility
Raw file and source integrity remain hash-based. Discrete scientific fields are compared with exact types and values, while only the declared posterior-risk diagnostic uses absolute tolerance 1e-14 and zero relative tolerance. Timings are reported separately. This avoids treating platform-dependent summation last bits as different experiments without relaxing action, budget or answer checks.
