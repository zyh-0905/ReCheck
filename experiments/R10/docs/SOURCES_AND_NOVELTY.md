# Sources and novelty boundary — checked 2026-09-14

This is a targeted primary-source review, not an exhaustive novelty proof. Recent papers below are preprints unless their own publication record states otherwise. No numerical result below is imported into our experiments.

1. Lilienthal & Hong, *Mind the Gap: Time-of-Check to Time-of-Use Vulnerabilities in LLM-Enabled Agents*, arXiv:2508.17155 (2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17155 and https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17155 . Already studies agent TOCTOU, monitoring and tool fusion. R10 does not claim to discover the read/use gap.
2. Lyu et al., *From Version Conflicts to Decision Conflicts: Selective Revalidation for Long-Running AI Agents*, arXiv:2609.08015v1 (2026-09-07). https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08015 . Explicit premises, localizing affected decisions and binding validated state to an effect are covered. In particular the paper explicitly distinguishes target-local CAS from external policy/binding dependencies. Its limitations include developer-authored predicates and controlled workflows. R10's selection-token example is NOT a new solution to that problem.
3. Reddy, Challaram & Basu, *Reason Less, Verify More: Deterministic Gates Recover a Silent Policy-Violation Failure Mode in Tool-Using LLM Agents*, arXiv:2607.07405. https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07405 . Shows task-success/tool-success separation and action-boundary gates in policy-permissive tools. R10 cannot claim that silent wrong state or deterministic gates are novel. We have inspected the paper, not replicated its benchmark or gates.
4. *Verified Detection and Prevention of Concurrency Anomalies in Multi-Agent Large Language Model Systems*, arXiv:2606.17182. https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17182 . Located via Exa; the retrieved text and associated public artifact describe stale-generation/lost-update anomalies and verified runtimes. We did not reproduce the formal verification. Treat as an additional close-work lead, not evidence for a claimed theorem here.
5. SQLite official transaction and isolation documentation: https://www.sqlite.org/lang_transaction.html ; https://www.sqlite.org/isolation.html . Separate connections observe committed changes; BEGIN IMMEDIATE controls a write transaction. R10 actually executes those statements and does not hold the transaction open during model generation. Deterministic event placement is not random concurrent-load measurement or a remote distributed-transaction guarantee.
6. DeepSeek official native tool and thinking-mode documentation: https://api-docs.deepseek.com/guides/tool_calls/ ; https://api-docs.deepseek.com/guides/thinking_mode/ . We reuse the previously qualified Chat Completions/native tools profile, preserve response reasoning fields as required, and link actual replies by ID. No strict beta endpoint, response_format, forced tool or extra model probe is added. Prior qualification is not a guarantee of all future responses or a weight identity certification.

## Actual R10 question
Does the observed R9NT1 pattern recur under a different local execution substrate, and do explicit target-re-resolution and minimal-business-write instructions change the *model's* use of the same strong legal tools? Two mechanisms are separated: selection changes outside the written row, and clobbering a protected field inside the written row. Test generic, minimal-only, intent-only and both without forcing the baseline to make full writes.

## Not claimed
A new concurrency theorem; optimal guard synthesis; global safety; restored data after arbitrary failure; independent real-world task distributions; official ToolSandbox/AgentDojo/tau-bench results; publication readiness. Strong simple re-resolution and standard conditional writes are baselines available to all arms. If they suffice, the result is retained rather than weakening them.
